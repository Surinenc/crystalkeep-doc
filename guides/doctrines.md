# Siege Doctrines

Twice in every run the game stops and asks you to choose. After you clear
**wave 2** and again after **wave 4**, three Siege Doctrines are offered and
you keep one. They last the rest of the run.

This is the only permanent, non-mana decision inside a run, and it is easy to
miss what it is really offering.

## How the choice works

- **Three** doctrines are drawn from a pool of **six**.
- Choose with **1**, **2** or **3**.
- You get **one reroll** per offer — a fresh three, from the same pool.
- A doctrine you already hold is never offered again, so the second choice is
  drawn from the five you did not take.

Both offers happen in the build phase, so you can look at your keep before
committing.

Offers come from their own random stream, seeded from the run seed and the
wave number alone. Nothing you did during the fight shifts them, and the
reroll is deterministic too — the same run, rerolled at the same point, shows
the same second set. You cannot fish for a doctrine by playing differently.

Doctrines are offered in **every mode** — Keep Assault, Endless Siege and the
Weekly Keep — on the same two waves. Endless does not keep handing them out as
it goes; a wave-40 Endless run has exactly the same two as a five-wave campaign.

They are not affected by the Weekly's power normalization either. Weekly pins
your gear, not your doctrines, so the choice matters more there than anywhere
else: it is one of the few advantages the mode still lets you build.

## What a doctrine actually is

Every doctrine gives one defense type its **Masterwork (Tier 3) mechanic at
any tier** — or grants a new interaction of comparable weight.

That is the part worth internalising. A doctrine is not a small percentage. It
is a 200-mana upgrade's worth of behaviour, applied to every defense of that
type you own and every one you build afterward, for free.

## The six

| Doctrine | Family | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Crosswind Bolts** | Ballista | Flank hits **fork** to a second enemy for 45% damage — the T3 mechanic, at any tier |
| **Rime Fracture** | Frost | Crushing a **Brittle** enemy releases a shatter pulse — the T3 Frost Rune mechanic, at any tier |
| **Oiled Gears** | Tar | Fire on an **oiled** enemy spreads the coating to a neighbour — the T3 Tar mechanic, at any tier |
| **Counter-Battery** | Artillery | Interrupting a Bombardier **detonates its charge**: 18 crush damage and a brief stagger to everything within ~3.2 m |
| **Keeper's Rhythm** | Wall | A Warden kill within **4.5 m** of a **wall** trap cuts **0.8 s** off its cooldown, and pays **+1 Ward** |
| **Living Rampart** | Blockade | A Blockade you **Mend** becomes a target enemies will attack |

### The three that unlock a tier mechanic

Crosswind Bolts, Rime Fracture and Oiled Gears all do the same thing in
different families: they hand you the Masterwork behaviour without the
Masterwork price.

Their value therefore depends entirely on what you have already built. Rime
Fracture on a keep with no Frost Rune is worth nothing; on a keep with three
Frost Runes feeding a Cannon line it is the strongest thing on offer. Read your
own board before you read the cards.

They also stack awkwardly with actually upgrading. If you were going to take
that Ballista to T3 anyway, Crosswind Bolts buys you nothing you were not
already paying for — take it only if it lets you *stop* upgrading and spend
the 200 mana elsewhere.

### The three that reward how you play

**Counter-Battery** turns an interrupt into an area attack. It scales with how
reliably you intercept Bombardiers — which is a Warden skill, not a build. If
you already interrupt artillery on instinct, this is free area damage on the
enemies that arrive with it.

**Keeper's Rhythm** rewards fighting *beside your own wall traps* rather than
in front of them. 0.8 s off a cooldown is significant on a trap that fires
every few seconds, and the Ward it pays feeds straight back into casting. It is
worthless if you built no wall traps.

**Living Rampart** is the strangest and the most interesting. Normally enemies
**never choose to attack a Blockade** — they compare the detour against
breaching and do one or the other. Living Rampart lets you Mend a Blockade to
make it a target they will willingly commit to, and they prefer it.

That is a taunt. It buys you a bait point of your own choosing: somewhere to
pull a column off the crystal, in front of the towers you already built. It is
the only doctrine that changes where the enemy goes rather than what happens
when it gets there.

## Choosing well

Ask, in order:

1. **Do I own the family?** Three of the six are dead cards on the wrong board.
2. **Was I going to buy that tier anyway?** If yes, the doctrine refunds you
   200 mana. If no, it gives you a mechanic you were never going to see.
3. **Does it reward something I actually do?** Counter-Battery and Keeper's
   Rhythm pay skilled players and ignore everyone else.

The first offer arrives after wave 2, when your board is small and your second
purse is unspent — so it is as much a statement of intent as a reward. Taking
Rime Fracture at wave 2 is a decision to build frost.
