# Getting started

Crystal Keep is a tower-defense and action-RPG hybrid. You read an unfamiliar
fortress, commit a small number of lanes, and personally hold the weak points
together while the plan survives contact.

## The loop

1. **Read the keep.** Route ribbons show each gate's path to the crystal. The
   tactical map (**Tab**) shows the whole thing at once.
2. **Build.** Spend mana on defenses. Mana is the only construction resource.
3. **Start the wave** with **G**. The game never starts one for you.
4. **Fight.** Hold the weak point, amplify your towers, intervene where the
   plan is failing.
5. **Read the debrief.** Find out which placements earned their cost.
6. **Repeat**, five times.

## Three things that will save your first run

**The crystal is the only defeat condition.** Your own death is a respawn
timer. Spend yourself on the lane that matters.

**Some matchups deal exactly zero.** A Ballista deals nothing through a
Shieldbearer's front. A Cannon deals nothing to a Fire Demon. When you see
`IMMUNE — 0` on screen, the answer is a different channel — not a bigger tower.
This is the single most common way a first run is lost.

**Mono-builds lose.** Ten ballistas is a build that has already surrendered to
wave 3. Mix damage channels.

## Starting resources

You begin with **400 mana**. Kills pay bounties — 14 for a goblin, 45 for an
orc, 420 for a Colossus — and each cleared wave pays a bonus. Selling a defense
returns **60%** of everything invested in it.

## Your first build

A reasonable opening on any keep:

- A **Blockade** (60) to shape where traffic walks
- A **Ballista** (100) covering the route, not sitting on it — remember the
  3.5 m minimum range
- A **Cannon** (125) for the crush channel you will need by wave 3

That leaves change for a reaction. Do not spend the whole purse before seeing a
wave.

## The Initiation

The Tutorial entry runs nine lessons that teach by doing rather than by
reading: reading the announced gate, following a route, placing and aiming,
starting a wave, changing the keep's topology and redeploying, the frontal
shield law, the Fire Demon law, Warden intervention, and claiming a reward.

Lessons six and seven exist specifically to teach the hard-zero laws, because
those are the rules that make an unprepared player lose without understanding
why.
